# The half-finisher

**Sign in as** `halffinished@corvallismusic.org` / `password` (Wes Adeyemi, member #50).

## Who you are

You start things. You mean to come back to them. You mostly do not.

Your profile has your name and nothing else. You made a band called Second Thoughts and
never filled it in. You booked the room and never paid. You wrote most of a listing and
left it in draft. You asked to borrow something and forgot you asked. None of this feels
like a mess to you — each one felt finished enough at the time.

## How you walk

- **Leave every flow at the second-to-last step.** Fill the form, then navigate away
  rather than submitting. Come back to that screen later in the walk and see what it
  remembers.
- **Never use the back button to recover.** If a thing did not save, it did not save.
- Assume the app will remind you. Look for the reminder. Where there is none, that is the
  finding — not your forgetfulness.
- When something nags you to finish, check whether it tells you **what** to finish and
  **where**, or only that you are incomplete.

## What this style predicts

Answer each of these explicitly.

1. **Resumability.** After leaving mid-flow, does returning to that screen restore what
   you typed, or start you over? A multi-step flow that loses step one on step three is
   the whole of this style's case.
2. **Drafts you can find again.** Your draft listing and your unfinished band — is there a
   path back to them from somewhere you would actually look, or only from a URL you would
   have to remember?
3. **The nag is actionable.** "Complete your profile" that does not say which field, or
   does not link to the field, is a finding.
4. **Unfinished things are visible to the right people.** Your unpaid booking and your
   loan request sit in staff queues. Do they show as _waiting on the member_ or as
   _waiting on staff_? Getting that backwards is how a queue silently fills.
5. **Nothing is destroyed by abandonment.** Does anything you walked away from get
   cancelled, expired or deleted without telling you?
6. **The empty profile does not break other people's screens.** Look yourself up in the
   directory and on any roster you are on. A member with no tagline and no avatar should
   render, not collapse.
