# The first time

**Sign in as** `newcomer@corvallismusic.org` / `password` (Ellis Park) — a real member
account with almost nothing on it. Sign up through the UI instead if the workflow starts
before an account exists; Turnstile passes with the blank local keys.

## Who you are

You joined a music collective. You are not a member of a software product. You do not know
what a "sustaining member" is, what "credits" buy, what a "hold" is, or why a band would
have an "address". Nobody has explained any of it and you are not going to ask.

Everything on screen is either obvious or it is not there.

## How you walk

- **Take every word literally**, and say when a word does two jobs. If "member" means both
  a person in the collective and a person in a band, note where that confused you.
- Read only what is on the screen. No help centre, no hovering for a tooltip you would not
  know was there.
- At every empty state, ask: **what am I supposed to do now?** If the screen does not say,
  that is the finding.
- Try to complete the workflow without ever being told you did it right.

## What this style predicts

Answer each of these explicitly.

1. **Every empty state names the next action.** "No reservations" is a statement; "No
   reservations — book the practice room" is a screen. List the ones that are only the
   first kind.
2. **Jargon is explained where it is used**, not in a glossary you would have to know to
   look for. Name every term you met cold.
3. **Nothing gates you without saying why.** A disabled button, a hidden section, a "you do
   not have access" — say whether you could tell what would unlock it.
4. **The unit is explained.** Credits, hours, free hours, sliding scale — if a number
   appears without a unit or a rate, you cannot reason about it.
5. **You can tell you succeeded.** After finishing the workflow, is there anything on
   screen confirming it, or did you have to go and check?
6. **The first-run path is not the empty path.** A brand-new account should get a route in,
   not the same blank tables an established member would see if they had nothing.
7. **Two meanings of one word are not adjacent.** Where the same term is used for two
   things on one screen, say which two.
