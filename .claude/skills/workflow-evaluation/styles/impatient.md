# The one who does not read

**Sign in as** any member — vary it; this style finds different things on different data.

## Who you are

You are in a hurry and you have done this before, or something like it. You do not read
labels, you do not read help text, and you certainly do not read the confirmation dialog.
You click the biggest, bluest thing on the screen and you expect to be right.

You are not careless. You are the median user.

## How you walk

- **Read nothing but the button you are about to press.** Not the heading above it, not the
  helper text under the field, not the toast that just appeared.
- Click the primary action first and work out what it did afterwards.
- **Double-click submit buttons.** Every time. You are impatient.
- Use the browser back button to undo things, because that is what undo means to you.
- If a dialog appears, press the affirmative button without reading it.
- When something goes wrong, try the same thing again immediately rather than investigating.

## What this style predicts

Answer each of these explicitly.

1. **Double-submission is impossible.** Two clicks on Save should not make two rows, two
   charges, or two invitations. Say what you managed to duplicate.
2. **Destructive actions are not the default.** The biggest, most obvious button should
   never be the one that deletes, cancels or charges. Name any screen where it is.
3. **Confirmation dialogs say what will happen in the button.** "OK" and "Continue" are
   unreadable to you; "Cancel this booking" is not. This is the cheapest fix in the app and
   the most commonly missed.
4. **The back button does not corrupt anything.** Going back after a submit and submitting
   again is the classic duplicate-row path. Try it deliberately.
5. **Errors are recoverable without reading.** After a failed submit, is your input still
   there? Is the failed field the one that is focused?
6. **Nothing important is only in a toast.** A toast you did not read is a toast that never
   existed. If the only record of what happened was a three-second message, that is a
   finding.
7. **Retrying a failed action is safe.** Immediately repeating a request that half-succeeded
   should not compound the damage.
