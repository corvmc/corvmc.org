## What it is for

CorvMC is member-driven, and this is where that happens in writing. **Suggestions**
in the member panel is a board of member ideas about anything the collective
does — gear we should buy, programming we should try, something about the space,
a policy, the website.

The point is comparison. An idea raised in the hallway is real but invisible;
one on the board can be counted, argued with, and answered in public.

## Posting an idea

Press **New Suggestion**, pick a category and write it up.

- **Website & Tools**, **Gear & Equipment**, **Events & Programming**,
  **The Space**, **Policy**, **Other**.
- Write the problem, not just the fix. "The practice room gets too hot in
  August" gets somewhere; "buy a fan" narrows it to one answer before anyone has
  weighed in.

Before posting, search — if the idea is already there, upvoting it counts for
more than a second copy of it.

## Voting

One vote per member per suggestion, and pressing it again takes it back. Sort by
**Top** to see what the room actually wants, or **New** to see what has just
landed.

Votes are the whole signal. Staff read the board sorted by them, so voting is not
decoration — it is how something moves.

## What the statuses mean

Staff set a status and, where it needs one, write a public response.

| Status          | What it means                                  |
| --------------- | ---------------------------------------------- |
| **Open**        | On the board, not yet decided                  |
| **Planned**     | We intend to do it                             |
| **In progress** | Somebody is working on it now                  |
| **Done**        | It happened                                    |
| **Declined**    | We are not doing it, and the response says why |

A declined suggestion stays on the board with its reason attached. Knowing why
something was turned down is worth more than the post quietly disappearing.

## Editing your own post

You can edit while nobody else has voted on it — those edits apply immediately.
Once somebody else has voted, your edit goes to staff for review first, because
people voted for what it said at the time.

Your own vote never locks your post.

## Duplicates

If two people post the same idea, staff merge them: the votes transfer and count
once per member, and the merged post points at the survivor. Nothing is lost and
nobody is punished for not having searched first.

## Reporting a suggestion

Every suggestion has a report action. Reporting pulls it off the board while
staff look at it, so use it for something that breaks the rules rather than for
an idea you disagree with — the vote is what disagreement is for.

If a report against something you posted is upheld, your later suggestions are
checked before they appear. See
[Reporting something, and what happens next](/member/help/reporting-and-standing).

## What this is not

It is not a ballot. There is no eligibility rule, no secrecy, and no closing
date — an upvote counter cannot do the job of a vote on a board election or a
budget, and this deliberately does not pretend to.
